Study Overview

Taxifolin inhibits amyloid-β fibril assembly and modulates cerebral amyloid angiopathy

In a 5XFAD mouse model of Alzheimer's disease, taxifolin was detected in cerebrospinal fluid — confirming that it crosses the blood-brain barrier. This is the single most important pharmacokinetic finding for DHQ's neuroprotective applications.

The study demonstrated that taxifolin inhibits the assembly of amyloid-β fibrils, the toxic aggregates responsible for neurodegeneration in Alzheimer's disease. Cerebral blood flow was restored in treated animals, and neuroinflammation markers (TNF-α, IL-1β) were significantly reduced.
